ResiCLAP: Semantic-Preserving Residual Enhancement for Fine-Grained Impact Sound Retrieval

CLAP retrieves the right kind of sound, but not the right one. ResiCLAP adds small, bounded corrections to a frozen CLAP embedding so that fine acoustic detail becomes searchable — without disturbing what CLAP already knows, and without leaving the ordinary single-vector retrieval setup.

The problem

A sound designer searching for a “metal impact” gets hundreds of results that are all, correctly, metal impacts. What separates a usable take from an unusable one is finer: how bright it is, how sharp the attack is, how long it rings. These properties are relative to the material — a bright metal impact and a bright wooden impact sit in very different absolute ranges — and audio–language embeddings tend to emphasise the gap between categories while under-expressing this variation within one.

Frozen backbone

Both CLAP encoders stay frozen. Only two small correction branches and their gates are trained — 0.37% of CLAP's parameters.

Bounded corrections

Each correction is steered sideways from the frozen embedding and capped in length, so the output can only be a small rotation of the original vector.

Drop-in retrieval

The output is still one normalised 512-dimensional vector. Cosine search over an unmodified CLAP index works unchanged.

How it works

ResiCLAP treats the frozen CLAP embedding as a fixed anchor and learns only bounded corrections around it. Two branches produce those corrections: one reads what CLAP already computed internally, the other re-reads the waveform at finer time resolution.

1. The attribute branch

Probing the frozen audio encoder shows that its intermediate layers carry fine acoustic detail far more strongly than the final embedding does. That detail is already computed, it is simply discarded by the time CLAP produces its output vector. The branch reads three of those intermediate signals directly:

AttributeWhere it is read fromSize
Brightnessearly layers, spectral-band activations384
Attackearly layers, onset activations96
Decaya late layer, ring-tail activations768

Each is mapped to the 512-dimensional CLAP space by a small two-layer network, and the three are merged by learned gates. Every gate starts almost closed, so each signal has to earn its influence during training.

2. The temporal branch

CLAP's internal time resolution is too coarse for transient detail, so this branch re-reads the waveform. From a 1.5 second window starting 100 milliseconds before the strike, it takes a fine-grained spectral-and-loudness description every 5 milliseconds.

Rather than reading those frames one by one, the branch compares pairs of time steps at four separations — 20, 40, 80 and 160 milliseconds — so it sees both the sharp transient and the longer ring in the same pass. Each attribute learns which separations matter to it.

3. Keeping CLAP intact

Each learned correction is constrained before it is added to the frozen CLAP embedding. This lets the model expose fine acoustic detail without replacing the semantic structure that makes CLAP useful for general audio search. The two corrections are added to the frozen embedding and the result is normalised back into a single retrieval vector. The paper gives the full formulation and ablation.

4. What the training rewards

The training objective balances learning fine acoustic detail with protecting CLAP's existing audio–text alignment:

Learning the fine detail

  • Match the enhanced embedding to a written description of its material and attribute.
  • Predict the measured attribute value.
  • Preserve the correct ordering between two sounds that differ in that attribute.

Protecting what CLAP knows

  • Penalise drift away from the frozen embedding.
  • Keep ordinary audio–text matching working on general audio.
  • Keep the enhanced model's similarity pattern close to frozen CLAP's.
