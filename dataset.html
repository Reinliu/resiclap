Dataset curation

Fine-grained retrieval needs pairs of sounds that are semantically identical but differ along exactly one perceptual axis. Natural recordings rarely isolate a single property, so we build those contrasts with signal processing — and then throw away most of what we make.

Why RealImpact

RealImpact provides controlled, repeatable impact recordings, which lets differences be measured rather than assumed. We keep one microphone observation per impact and use 34 objects spanning four materials: ceramic, metal, plastic and wood. Each recording is analysed for three properties:

PropertyMeasured by
Brightnessspectral centroid
Attackonset rise time
DecayT20-like energy-decay time

The generate-then-filter pipeline

1

Analyse

Measure brightness, attack and decay on every base recording.

2

Generate

Render DSP candidates that push one property up or down.

3

Re-analyse

RMS-match, peak-limit, then measure all three properties again.

4

Filter

Keep a candidate only if it passes every acceptance test.

How candidates are generated

Brightness

Second-order high-shelf filter, 2 kHz corner, gains of ±{3, 6, 9, 12} dB.

Attack

Time-warping from onset to envelope peak with exponents {0.5 … 0.0625} for sharper and {1.5 … 5.0} for softer, plus envelope reshaping over the first 40 ms. Sharper variants add a transient term; softer variants use sin² fade-ins of 5, 10 or 20 ms.

Decay

Post-onset envelope modification: exponential damping with τ ∈ {30, 60, 120, 250} ms, or raised-cosine tail fades starting at 50, 100 or 200 ms.

Acceptance criteria

A candidate survives only if it (a) moves the intended property in the requested direction, (b) stays within the plausible range for its category, and (c) leaves the other two properties largely unchanged. Across 60,000 generated candidates, 31,413 were retained.

  • 15,464 — failed a combined quality check
  • 11,593 — changed the other two properties too much
  • 1,530 — property did not move in the requested direction

Decay is the hard case. Decay candidates are generated, but modifying a post-onset envelope without also disturbing brightness proved difficult: every decay candidate was ultimately rejected by the cross-property test, so the retained corpus contains brightness and attack variants only. Decay is therefore learned from the measured decay of real recordings rather than from synthesised contrasts — which is consistent with our finding that decay shows the smallest cross-corpus gain of the three properties.

The retained corpus contains 10,000 originals and 31,413 variants, for 41,413 clips in total. Text pairs mirror this structure, combining the material with the adjective pairs bright/dull, sharp/soft and long ringing/short dry.

What the reported model uses

The curated corpus above is the full augmentation resource. The residual branches reported in the paper are trained on a probe-aligned subset of base RealImpact recordings, each carrying category-relative labels for all three properties, mixed with general audio so that the preservation objectives have something to preserve.

Training batches mix RealImpact, Clotho and FSD50K in a 50 / 25 / 25 ratio; the Clotho and FSD50K portions carry the semantic-preservation and distillation terms.
